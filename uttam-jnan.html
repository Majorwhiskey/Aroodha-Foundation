His Life in Brief

Four movements of a search

೧

A curiosity that would not settle

From a young age, Uttam Jnan was driven by an insatiable curiosity to uncover the truth about life. Not the borrowed answers of habit or convention — the thing itself. It made him a difficult student and a relentless reader, and it did not let him rest.

೨

The relentless pursuit

That pursuit of knowledge and understanding took him through texts, teachers, and long stretches of solitary practice. He tested what he read against what he could actually experience, and discarded whatever failed the test. Much of it failed.

೩

Meeting the guru

The search led him to a divine guru — and that meeting transformed his life forever. What had been a search became a discipline; what had been curiosity became certainty of a quieter kind. He speaks of it plainly: everything he now teaches, he received there.

೪

Founding Aroodha

Aroodha Foundation grew out of the wish to pass that on without dilution. He teaches personally — morning practice, weekly satsang, kriya, and mantra diksha — and writes for those who cannot come in person. He takes no fee for the teaching itself.

Neti, Neti • ನೇತಿ ನೇತಿ

Not this, not this

The Upanishads’ way of inquiry is to set aside, one by one, everything that can be observed — thoughts, roles, memories, even the voice that narrates them.

Scroll, and let each one go. What cannot be set aside is what you are.
